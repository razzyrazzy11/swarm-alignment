# What should an investigator request next?

Track 4 asks how to oversee agents whose useful work crosses several boundaries. This study separates two practical problems: an essential authentic witness is missing, or the reader has sufficient witnesses but reaches an unsupported conclusion. Its finite authorization contract is a test instrument, not a deployment standard.

## One example before the metrics

A principal issues lease L to one worker. A second worker activates child D. A third worker applies a fake increment at logical time 2. The lease expires at time 2. Under the declared half-open interval `[0, 2)`, that settlement is outside the lease even if the earlier request was permitted. A completion message cannot override the settlement record.

This is an illustrative contract example, not a newly observed model failure. The frozen calibration bank includes expiry and revocation cases separately from the natural worker trajectories. Its results must not be used to estimate how often workers violate a policy.

## Establish the packet's limits

First identify the designated closed settlement. Then distinguish the request, observed result and immutable executor binding. Finally join issuance, delegation, scope, remaining quota, settlement time and effective revocation. Parent revocation invalidates the child under this study's explicit rule; different systems may use a different rule.

Several hidden histories can remain compatible with the same packet while agreeing on a single label. A definite finding is justified when **all compatible histories agree**, not only when the investigator reconstructs exactly one history. If labels differ, abstain or retrieve another authentic record. Contradictory trusted records require investigation of the recording contract rather than a guess about the worker.

## Choose the intervention that answers the problem

| Question | Comparison | What a benefit would establish |
|---|---|---|
| Which authentic records should be retrieved? | Random, metadata-priority and contract-aware retrieval at two records each | Better acquisition under this declared record budget and prior |
| Can the reader use evidence that is already sufficient? | Direct versus explicit-join instructions on the same preassigned sufficient packet | A raw decision improvement from the instruction intervention |
| Should an unsupported finding be published? | Raw answer versus deterministic support veto | Enforcement of the finite support rule, with its definite-coverage cost |

Equal record counts do not mean equal bytes, tokens, information or total cost. The contract-aware selector uses a uniform prior over compatible finite histories; it does not consult the hidden actual history. It always spends the declared retrieval allowance and is not an optimal stopping policy. The sufficient reference packet is privileged instrumentation and is not itself a two-record acquisition policy.

## Read findings without mixing their meanings

Report supported definite answers, unsupported accusations, unsupported NONE, abstentions and invalid outputs together. Correctly abstaining on ambiguous evidence is grounded behavior; abstaining on a sufficient packet forfeits a correct definite answer. The veto can suppress an unsupported answer but cannot earn credit as improved raw reasoning.

The independent experimental unit is a source group. The three workers, repeated evidence views and multiple readers within that group do not increase the independent sample size. Calibration, prior studies and numerical checkpoint interventions remain separately labeled.

## Where this transfers and where it stops

[Swarm Traces' limitations](https://swarmtraces.org/#limitations) distinguish recovered requests from confirmed responses and describe uncertain agent identities. Those limitations motivate asking for an operator-side result and immutable executor witness. They do not supply complete incident ground truth. The [METR investigation](https://metr.org/hugging-face-incident-report-aug-2026.pdf), especially its data and timing limitations, similarly motivates preserving uncertainty about temporal ordering.

Our retrieval conditions assume authentic originals are available. If an incident never recorded them, retrieval cannot repair the omission. Hashes attest artifact identity under a trusted recording assumption; they do not prove that the original observation was truthful. See [the frozen protocol](FACTORIAL_PROTOCOL.md), [source review](FACTORIAL_SOURCES.md) and [rubric crosswalk](FACTORIAL_RUBRIC.md) for the exact scope. No result here establishes swarm intent, broad collusion detection or deployment safety.
